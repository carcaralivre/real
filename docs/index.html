The US dollar in Brazilian reais since the Real Plan, adjusted by the IPCA (Brazil's consumer price index) to today's money. The rate of the day is misleading: R$ 3.81 in 2002 weighed far more on the wallet than R$ 6 in 2024.

Chart of the US dollar in Brazilian reais since 1994, nominal and inflation-adjusted.

How to read it

  • IPCA-adjusted: each month's average rate multiplied by Brazil's accumulated inflation since then. It shows what that dollar would cost in today's reais.
  • Net of US inflation: the dollar also loses purchasing power. This line removes that effect and is the fairest measure of "really expensive" (the real exchange rate).
  • Nominal: the rate as it appeared in the newspapers at the time.
  • Last point: the most recent daily rate (PTAX selling rate), unadjusted. The other points are monthly averages, so single-day spikes are smoothed out.
  • Events: the context most often cited for each move, not proof of cause. The line between Dilma and Temer is drawn in May 2016, when she was suspended from office.

Sources

  • Central Bank of Brazil, SGS: series 3698 (USD/BRL, monthly average), 1 (USD/BRL, daily rate) and 433 (monthly IPCA).
  • FRED, series CPIAUCSL: US consumer price index. Unpublished months are interpolated.
